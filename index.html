INTRODUCTION

More than reaching a summit.

Mountaineering brings together exploration, technical skill, endurance, judgment and respect for changing mountain conditions.

The activity ranges from trekking and alpine climbing to demanding high-altitude expeditions. The environment can be unpredictable, so responsible preparation and conservative decision-making are central to the experience.

SAFETY

Know the risks.

Mountain hazards change with terrain, season and weather. The safest plan is one that allows conditions to change without forcing the team to continue.

Avalanches

Assess snowpack, terrain and current conditions.

Weather

Storms, wind and rapid temperature changes can reshape plans.

Crevasses

Glaciated terrain requires route awareness and appropriate systems.

Altitude

Acclimatization, pacing and monitoring are essential at elevation.

SAFETY FIRSTTrain for the terrain, check conditions, communicate clearly, and turn around when the risk no longer matches the plan.

GUIDELINES

Prepare before you climb.

These are general educational guidelines, not a substitute for professional instruction, local conditions or emergency advice.

01

Before the expedition

  • Check the weather and route conditions.
  • Prepare and inspect appropriate equipment.
  • Plan the route and realistic turnaround points.
  • Inform emergency contacts and understand communications.
  • Get training appropriate to the terrain.
02

During climbing

  • Follow agreed team safety procedures.
  • Stay with the group and communicate changes.
  • Monitor weather, terrain and personal condition.
  • Use equipment according to training and manufacturer guidance.
03

Emergency preparation

  • Carry reliable communication and navigation tools.
  • Know the nearest rescue resources where possible.
  • Maintain first-aid preparedness.
  • Have a clear emergency and turnaround plan.
